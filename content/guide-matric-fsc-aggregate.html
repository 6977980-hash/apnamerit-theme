The three steps

  1. Turn each part into a percentage: marks obtained ÷ total marks × 100. Matric 1000/1100 = 90.91%; FSc 950/1100 = 86.36%; FSc Part-I 480/550 = 87.27%. The percentage calculator does this for any board total.
  2. Multiply each percentage by its weight in your university's formula (for example 10% Matric and 40% FSc for MDCAT).
  3. Add the weighted parts. If the formula has an entry test, add the test part the same way (test score ÷ test total × weight).

Worked example: Matric 1000, FSc 950

Take a student with Matric 1000/1100 (90.91%), FSc 950/1100 (86.36%) and FSc Part-I 480/550 (87.27%):

  • Punjab University, basic criteria: 20% × 90.91 + 80% × 86.36 = 18.18 + 69.09 = 87.27%. PU writes the same formula as (Matric ÷ 4 + FSc) ÷ 1375: (250 + 950) ÷ 1375 = 87.27%.
  • IIUI, no test: 40% × 90.91 + 60% × 86.36 = 36.36 + 51.82 = 88.18%.
  • MDCAT: Matric and FSc give 9.09 + 34.55 = 43.64 of the 50 points they can give. With 150/180 in MDCAT (41.67 points) the aggregate is 85.30%.
  • UET Lahore: 17% × 90.91 + 50% × 87.27 = 15.45 + 43.64 = 59.09 points; with 250/400 in ECAT (20.63 points) the aggregate is 79.72%.
  • NUST: Matric and Part-I give only 22.18 points; with 70% in NET (52.50 points) the aggregate is 74.68%, which is why NET decides NUST merit.

The same marks give 74.68% at NUST and 88.18% at IIUI, so never compare an aggregate from one university with another university's closing merit.
