MBBS and BDS closing merit {data_year} in Punjab (UHS)

The table shows the closing merit of every Punjab medical and dental college that admits through the University of Health Sciences (UHS), Lahore. Closing merit is the aggregate of the last open-merit student admitted to that college in that selection list. By default you see the final list, open merit, {data_year}; use the filters to see the 1st list or 2024. Private BDS shows the 3rd list, the latest UHS has published for that sector.

How did MBBS merit change from 2024 to 2025?

Public-sector MBBS closing merits in Punjab fell by about 0.7 to 1.2 percentage points in 2025. King Edward Medical University closed at 96.06% in the 17th (last) 2024 list and at 94.87% in the final 2025 list; Narowal Medical College, the lowest, went from 94.24% to 93.51%. The "Change vs previous year" column shows the difference for each public college. Closing merits move with the difficulty of each year's MDCAT, so treat the change as a guide, not a rule.

Public MBBS merit list 2025: top and bottom

  • Highest: King Edward Medical University (KEMC), Lahore, 94.87%; Allama Iqbal Medical College, Lahore, 94.45%; Services Institute of Medical Sciences, Lahore, 94.23%.
  • Lowest: Narowal Medical College, 93.51%; D.G. Khan Medical College, 93.53%; Sheikh Zayed Medical College, Rahim Yar Khan, 93.54%.

All 17 public medical colleges closed within about 1.4 points of each other, so a small difference in MDCAT marks decides which college you get.

Private MBBS and BDS closing merit 2025

Private college merits spread much wider. In the 5th and final 2025 private MBBS list, Al-Aleem Medical College, Lahore closed highest at 90.84% and the lowest open-merit closing was 61.00%. Public BDS colleges closed between 93.42% and 93.46% in the 13th list, while private BDS colleges ranged from 73.13% down to 55.87% in the 3rd list.

1st merit list vs final merit list

Merit drops after the 1st list because students who got a seat move up to colleges they prefer, or leave for other programs. In 2025, KEMU went from 94.92% in the 1st list to 94.87% in the final list, while private MBBS colleges dropped by much more as seats were refilled. Use the "Merit list" filter to compare.
