For session 2026-27, follow the UHS MBBS/BDS admission schedule and read how to check the UHS provisional merit list before the selection lists start.

How did MBBS merit change from 2024 to 2025?

Public-sector MBBS closing merits in Punjab fell by about 0.7 to 1.2 percentage points in 2025. King Edward Medical University closed at 96.06% in the 17th (last) 2024 list and at 94.87% in the final 2025 list; Narowal Medical College, the lowest, went from 94.24% to 93.51%. The "Change vs previous year" column shows the difference for each public college. Closing merits move with the difficulty of each year's MDCAT, so treat the change as a guide, not a rule.

Public MBBS merit list 2025: top and bottom

  • Highest: King Edward Medical University (KEMC), Lahore, 94.87%; Allama Iqbal Medical College, Lahore, 94.45%; Services Institute of Medical Sciences, Lahore, 94.23%.
  • Lowest: Narowal Medical College, 93.51%; D.G. Khan Medical College, 93.53%; Sheikh Zayed Medical College, Rahim Yar Khan, 93.54%.

All 17 public medical colleges closed within about 1.4 points of each other, so a small difference in MDCAT marks decides which college you get.

MBBS and BDS seats, reserved seats and fee 2026-27

For session 2026-27 the UHS prospectus lists 4,151 MBBS seats in 19 Punjab public medical colleges (3,796 on open merit) and 340 BDS seats in 5 dental colleges (285 on open merit), including two new medical colleges and two dental colleges still subject to PM&DC approval. The closing merits on this page are for the open-merit seats.

[am_seats uni="uhs"]

Reserved seats are filled on separate merit lists: students with disabilities (after a UHS medical board), the 54 MBBS seats for seven under-developed districts (domicile and class 1 to 12 schooling in the district, and a bond to serve there five years), one Cholistan seat at Sheikh Zayed Medical College, Overseas Pakistanis and dual nationals (US$ 10,000 a year), and seats for candidates of other provinces and regions. Rules are in the prospectus (pp. 40-44).

Private MBBS and BDS closing merit 2025

Private college merits spread much wider. In the 5th and final 2025 private MBBS list, Al-Aleem Medical College, Lahore closed highest at 90.84% and the lowest open-merit closing was 61.00%. Public BDS colleges closed between 93.42% and 93.46% in the 13th list, while private BDS colleges ranged from 73.13% down to 55.87% in the 3rd list. Each college is listed on the private medical colleges merit list and the BDS merit list.

1st merit list vs final merit list

Merit drops after the 1st list because students who got a seat move up to colleges they prefer, or leave for other programs. In 2025, KEMU went from 94.92% in the 1st list to 94.87% in the final list, while private MBBS colleges dropped by much more as seats were refilled. Use the "Merit list" filter to compare.

MDCAT closing merit position (merit number)

Besides the aggregate, UHS gives every candidate a merit number in its order of merit. The "Closing position" column shows the merit number of the last open-merit student admitted to each college: we matched the student holding the closing aggregate in the selection list with UHS's Final Order of Merit for that sector. No names or IDs are kept.

  • Public MBBS, final 2025 list: King Edward Medical University closed at merit number 440 (399 in the 1st list), Allama Iqbal Medical College at 946 and Services Institute of Medical Sciences at 1,447. The last public college, Narowal Medical College, closed at 3,516.
  • Public BDS: 1,513 to 1,619 in the BDS order of merit (final list). The BDS order is a separate list, and most students above these numbers took an MBBS seat.
  • Private MBBS, final list: from 777 (Al-Aleem Medical College) to 7,859 (Queens Medical College, Kasur) in the private-sector order of merit.

When UHS publishes the new provisional merit list, compare your merit number with these positions: a smaller number than a college's closing position means you were ahead of last year's last admitted student. The number of seats and of students changes each year, so it is a guide, not a promise.
