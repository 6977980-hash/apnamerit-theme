Order by what you want, not by last year's merit

Because allotment is merit-cum-preference, putting a high-merit college first never costs you a lower one: if your rank does not reach your first choice, the system moves to your second, third and so on. So the only question is which college would you rather attend. List the colleges you genuinely prefer first (city, hostel, distance from home, family), and the ones you would accept only if nothing else works last. Do not skip a college you would still take, because a skipped college is gone for good.

A simple three-group method

  1. Reach: colleges whose last final-list closing merit is above your aggregate by up to about 1.5 points. Put the ones you want most at the top; upgradation lists sometimes reach them.
  2. Match: colleges within 1.5 points either side of your aggregate. This is where you are most likely to land in the first list; order them by preference.
  3. Safe: colleges whose closing merit is clearly below your aggregate. Order them too; they are your fallback if the year's merit rises.

The merit predictor does this grouping for you. Enter your MDCAT aggregate, choose UHS Punjab, and it prints a suggested preference order (highest closing merit first, grouped Strong, Borderline and Unlikely) that you can copy and then rearrange within each group by your own preference. Every college's last closing merit, with the official list behind it, is on the MDCAT merit list.

What last year's numbers say

In the final 2025 UHS list the 17 public MBBS colleges closed within 1.36 points of each other (King Edward 94.87%, Narowal 93.51%), and the four public BDS colleges within 0.05 points. That narrow band means your order matters a great deal: two students with the same aggregate can land in colleges 300 km apart purely because of how they ordered the middle of their list.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out far-away colleges "to avoid being sent there". If your rank only reaches those colleges, leaving them out means no seat at all, not a nearer seat.
  • Ordering by last year's merit instead of by preference. Merit order is only useful as a reality check for where you will probably land.
  • Expecting to change the order after the deadline, or to decline an upgradation in a public college. Neither is allowed.
  • Applying for MBBS and BDS on the same form. They are separate portals, separate fees and separate preference lists.
