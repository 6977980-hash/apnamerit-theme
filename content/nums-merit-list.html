These are the closing merits of the private medical and dental colleges affiliated with the National University of Medical Sciences (NUMS), session 2025-26: the aggregate of the last candidate in the newest selection list NUMS has published for each college (the sixth MBBS list of 27 January 2026 and the fourth BDS list of 13 February 2026). NUMS does not say these were the last lists, so a later list could have closed a little lower.

  • QIMS Baluchistan-domicile seats closed at 81.59%, lower than its open-merit seats.

NUMS merit 2024-25 vs 2025-26

For session 2024-25, NUMS issued twelve MBBS selection lists. We read each college's 12th list (February 2025), whose PDFs are still on numspak.edu.pk; pick the year 2024 in the filter above. MBBS open merit closed between 88.36% (QIMS Quetta) and 83.44% (KIMS Karachi), with CMH Lahore at 86.00%. Most colleges closed lower in 2025-26: CMH Lahore 85.32%, CMH Bahawalpur 84.80% (86.07% in 2024-25), QIMS 86.73%. CMH Kharian, HITEC, KIMS and Wah closed slightly higher. For BDS 2024-25 only the 1st list prints aggregates (CMH Lahore 84.45%, QCD Quetta 87.30%), so later 2024-25 BDS lists may have closed lower.

How is the NUMS aggregate calculated?

NUMS colleges use the PM&DC formula: 50% MDCAT, 40% FSc and 10% Matric. Work yours out with the MDCAT aggregate calculator, then compare it with this table, or with the UHS Punjab MDCAT merit list for public and private colleges in Punjab.

Why are the NUMS merits lower than UHS public colleges?

These are private-sector colleges with higher fees, so fewer top scorers choose them; UHS public MBBS colleges closed between 93.51% and 94.87% in 2025. UHS private MBBS colleges, a closer comparison, closed between 61.00% and 90.84% in the 2025 final list.
