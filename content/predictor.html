How does the merit predictor work?

For each program we take the closing merit, the aggregate of the last student admitted, from the final official list of the newest year we hold for that program (open merit). That one figure is the reference, and its year is printed next to it. Then:

  • Strong: your aggregate is more than 1.5 points above the reference.
  • Borderline: within 1.5 points either side.
  • Unlikely: more than 1.5 points below.

The full rule is on our methodology page. Merit changes every year with seats, applicants and the test, so treat the result as a guide, not a promise.

Which aggregate should I enter?

Each university calculates merit with its own formula, so an MDCAT aggregate cannot be compared with a PU or UET figure. Work out your aggregate first, then pick the same university in the predictor:

  • MBBS / BDS: MDCAT aggregate calculator (50% MDCAT, 40% FSc, 10% Matric), compared with the MDCAT merit list.
  • NUMS private MBBS / BDS: the same MDCAT aggregate, compared with the NUMS merit list.
  • UET Lahore: UET aggregate calculator, compared with the UET merit list (A1 open merit, Fourth Merit List).
  • Punjab University: PU aggregate calculator, compared with the PU merit list.
  • UET Taxila: UET Taxila aggregate calculator, compared with the UET Taxila merit list.
  • COMSATS: COMSATS aggregate calculator, compared with the COMSATS merit list (Lahore and Islamabad campuses).
  • Karachi University: academic percentage as KU publishes it, compared with the KU merit list.

Aggregates you calculate on this device appear as buttons at the top of the predictor, so one tap fills the right number. All calculators are listed on the aggregate calculators page and all closing lists on the merit lists page.
